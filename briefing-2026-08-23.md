- *ML-guided methane-separation MOFs* — **Primary / university source.** The University of Chicago team reports an end-to-end workflow that used machine learning, experimental synthesis, and industry-facing design to produce zinc-based UCHI-1 and UCHI-2 materials for methane/nitrogen separation. The operator lesson is not “AI discovered a climate material”; it is that lower-cost feedstocks and manufacturability constraints were included before the handoff to scale.[1]
- *Metastable polymer enables circular light-based 3D printing* — **Primary / university source plus peer-reviewed paper.** Heidelberg researchers report a printable polymer network that can be chemically disassembled into building blocks and re-polymerized, while retaining the demonstrated print quality and mechanical function. This is a controlled materials result, not evidence of an industrial recycling system: solvent recovery, trigger recovery, contamination, and repeated-cycle performance still need process-level validation.[2]
- *Electrochemical ammonia catalyst screening* — **Primary / MIT source.** MIT researchers describe a computational approach for identifying promising catalyst materials for electrochemical ammonia production, aimed at alternatives to fossil-intensive Haber–Bosch processing. The important change is better candidate selection; the source does not establish economic competitiveness, long-duration operation, or industrial throughput.[3]
- *Continuous flotation for silver from end-of-life PV* — **Primary preprint / independent research team.** The ChemRxiv preprint reports a continuous flotation step that maintained near-complete silver recovery during a pilot-scale run and reached approximately technology-readiness level 5. Because this is a preprint and the economic assessment is preliminary, the next proof point is sustained operation on heterogeneous panels with a full reagent, water, residue, and downstream-refining account.[5]
- *Canada funds chemistry and resource-validation infrastructure* — **Primary / Canada Foundation for Innovation.** CFI announced more than $118 million for 357 research-infrastructure projects at 68 postsecondary institutions, including molecular reaction imaging in Ontario and a Tailings, Ore, Water laboratory for cleaner extraction and critical minerals. This is enabling capacity, not a demonstrated chemistry result, but it creates concrete partnership surfaces for GreenChemistry.ai and university/industrial validation programs.[6]

Do not label the output “green hydrogen” without a fossil-carbon and full-system-boundary analysis.[9]

:factory: *Builder Practice / Workflow*
Build a **chemistry-to-deployment evidence packet** for each candidate route.[1][2][5]
1. Freeze the feedstock and impurity envelope before optimization.[1][5][8]
2. Track mass balance, yield/selectivity, energy, water, solvent, catalyst or reagent loss, and residue fate in the same run record.[5][7][9]
3. Test the process in a representative reactor or continuous unit before making scale claims.[5][7][10]
4. Add a recovery/reuse loop and run repeated cycles, not just first-pass performance.[2][4][5]
5. Store raw instrument data, method versions, model versions, assumptions, and human decisions in a linked ELN/LIMS record.[1][3]
6. Require an independent LCA/TEA review before converting a lab result into a commercial or environmental claim.[5][7][9]
